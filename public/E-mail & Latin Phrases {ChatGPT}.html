Let’s go into the historical and cultural context of both phrases: Fortis Fortuna Adiuva - Meaning & Origin: Translated as “Fortune favors the brave”, this phrase highlights the idea that those who act boldly are more likely to succeed. The Latin fortis means “brave” or “strong,” Fortuna is the Roman goddess of luck, and adiuva means “helps.” - Roman Context: The Romans often linked military success and personal achievement with Fortuna. Soldiers and leaders believed that bravery invited the goddess’s favor, while hesitation invited misfortune. - Cultural Legacy: Variations of this phrase appear throughout history. For example, in the works of Terence and Virgil, similar sentiments arise. In modern times, it’s used as a motto by military units, schools, and even popularized in media (such as the John Wick series). Ad astra per aspera - Meaning & Origin: Literally “To the stars through hardships.” It’s a metaphor for reaching lofty goals (the stars) by persevering through struggles (aspera = difficulties, roughness). - Roman & Literary Roots: While not a classical Roman motto, it reflects Stoic and heroic ideals in Latin literature. The phrase embodies the notion that greatness requires struggle. - Modern Usage: - It’s the official state motto of Kansas (USA), symbolizing overcoming trials to achieve freedom and progress. - Widely adopted in aerospace, science, and space exploration communities as an inspirational motto. - Appears in memorials and educational institutions as a call to resilience. In essence: - Fortis Fortuna Adiuva = bravery attracts success. - Ad astra per aspera = greatness requires struggle.
